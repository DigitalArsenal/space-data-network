1Summary

Space Data Network (SDN) builds a catalog of anthropogenic space objects (ASOs) from public and login-only sources. Object by object, it publishes the solution with the best measured accuracy for the intended use, with its uncertainty. Accuracy is measured against independent precise orbits, under plans fixed before the test data were read1. Each method is chosen and tuned on one set of data, the selection window (also called the training window), and scored on held-out data: observations set aside until the method is fixed, either a later time window or objects the tuning never saw. A result on held-out data shows how the method performs on cases it has not seen, not how well it fits the data it was tuned on. Section 2 states the method step by step, with the rule that gives every object a covariance.

Object class Catalog product Measured (median)
GPS satellites Analysis-center orbit, propagated 3.5 cm at issue, 30 m a day later
Satellites with laser ranging High-precision orbit propagation (HPOP) fitted to the ranges 7 m at the fit, 42 m after 7 days
Satellites with operator ephemerides The operator's ephemeris Public element sets (OMMs) lie 0.6–5 km from it
Objects known only from public OMMs SGP4 on the OMM 0.3–1.6 km at issue

For most objects the only public input is the OMM, and the catalog takes it as it is. What the catalog adds is a measured account of each input's error, and screening that acts on it safely:

  1. Measured error as covariance. An orbit propagated from an OMM's epoch state carries a covariance equal to the error measured for its orbit regime and element-set age. That error is measured against independent orbits in every regime that has them: GNSS analysis-center orbits, laser ranging, LEO precise orbits, operator ephemerides and radio-occultation receivers. A covariance is labeled calibrated only where held-out data confirm it (section 2.2).
  2. SDN's own atmosphere. Drag uses JB2008 or NRLMSISE-00 with an open correction in the form of the Space Force's Dynamic Calibration of the Atmosphere, solved from public operator states. It explains 25–40 % of the density variation measured by satellites that did not calibrate it; 1–3-day position error is unchanged (ratios 0.98–1.02) (section 5.4).
  3. Maneuvers. A detector reads each object's element-set history for steps in orbital energy and plane, and reports each maneuver's time and size. On held-out data it found 22 of 25 ISS burns and all 6 GPS maneuvers in precise orbits; geostationary station-keeping is harder, 4 of 11 (section 5.5).
  4. Better start states where data allow. HPOP's force models agree with Orekit within 1 cm over a day (steps of at most 30 s; 2.5 cm at 300 s), and with GMAT, Tudat, Nyx and Basilisk within the same agreement limits once each tool's atmosphere settings match (section 7). Fitted to laser ranges, HPOP stays 20–60 times better than SGP4 to 7 days; fitted to an arc of corrected OMMs, it halves SGP4's error to two days in GPS (section 5.3). In LEO, drag from B* limits even a precise start state: 481 m at 24 h (section 5.3). SGP4-XP sets that SDN fits to precise GNSS orbits err by a median 0.42 km over the seventh day, against 14 km for its SGP4 fits of the same orbits (exploratory, section 4).
  5. Screening when the covariance is large. A large covariance spreads a collision's probability thin. In a synthetic study, with collisions built on the OMM errors of 4 reference objects, a probability threshold of 1e-4 missed 2.7–16 % of collisions; screening on the uncertainty region missed 0.2–0.4 %, with 21–45 % false alerts at a 1 km miss50. Every event also carries the Alfano maximum, the largest probability a spherical covariance of any size gives, which needs no covariance. Products built from public OMMs are advisory: public element sets alone must not drive conjunction assessment decisions49,53.

Three results go beyond published work:

  1. An element-set correction learned on a LEO fleet carries to that fleet's unseen satellites (25 % lower error at epoch) but not to other operators' satellites, nor across GPS orbital planes; in GNSS a per-satellite bias holds 37 % of the along-track variance, against 1–5 % in LEO (section 5.3).
  2. A density correction of the kind the Space Force's drag model uses, solved from Planet's public states, is scored on satellites that did not calibrate it (section 5.4).
  3. Consecutive OMM errors are strongly correlated, and four published covariance constructions are overconfident (section 6).

Pre-registered hypotheses that failed are reported as not supported.

2Method

The catalog is assembled object by object from the products of every source. Each object ends with a state, a covariance and its maneuvers.

2.1Construction

  1. Read each source at its level. Each record becomes the Space Data Standards (SDS) record of its kind, under the source's own identifier: element sets as OMMs, ephemerides as OEMs, laser normal points and other measurements as observations (sections 3 and 4).

  2. Derive the same products from every source. For every object a source covers, the method derives four records:

    1. An SGP4 element set (OMM): the source's own, or one fitted to the source's ephemeris (section 4).
    2. An SGP4-XP element set: an OMM in the extended-perturbation theory of the Space Force's Astrodynamics Standards52, with its ballistic and radiation coefficients, fitted to the same data (section 4).
    3. A state with its covariance (OCM): the source's state at its epoch, or the SGP4 epoch state of the handoff in section 4, with the covariance of section 2.2.
    4. The maneuvers in the source's history (MNV), from the detector of section 5.5.

    Sources are then compared like for like, and each can be propagated and shown on its own.

  3. Measure each source. Each product is scored against independent precise orbits by object class, orbit regime and product age, under a plan fixed before its test data were read (sections 5 and 10). A source's measured errors form its error model.

  4. Associate. Crosswalks propose links between records of one object; ephemerides compared over shared intervals accept, reject or hold each link (section 8).

  5. Select. For each object, regime and horizon, the catalog takes the product of the source with the lowest measured median error on a training window of at least 30 samples. Fusing sources was never better than selecting one (section 8).

  6. Attach covariance and maneuvers. The selected product keeps its covariance and label (section 2.2). The object carries the maneuvers of all its sources, merged by time and size. A product whose epoch precedes a maneuver does not describe the orbit after it, so selection after a maneuver draws only on later products.

  7. Write the catalog. A catalog record (CAT) per object, with its selected OMM, SGP4-XP OMM and OCM and its merged MNV records, each naming the source it came from.

2.2Covariance for every object

Every object carries a covariance, from the first rule that applies:

  1. The operator supplies one. The operator's covariance, as supplied.
  2. An orbit fit made the product. The fit's formal covariance, scaled by the realism factor measured for that source and object class on held-out data. Unscaled, laser-range fits are 16–21 times too small in mean d²/3 (section 6).
  3. The source has an error model for the object's regime. The source's measured error at the element set's age, mapped from radial, in-track and cross-track axes to Cartesian. The model is built from the errors of consecutive element sets against independent precise orbits, with their correlation, and calibrated by regime and age (section 6). AOE's sets match Space-Track's error at equal age (section 4) and take its model.
  4. Otherwise. The same model pooled over all sources in the object's regime.

Rules 3 and 4 cover every object with an element set, so no object goes without a covariance.

Each covariance carries a label: the rule, the source, the regime and age cell, and the verdict of held-out data for that cell. Calibrated means the realism rule of section 6 held on held-out data: mean d²/3 in [0.8, 1.25] and 95 % coverage in [0.93, 0.97]. Otherwise the label records the measured mean d²/3 and coverage. The element-set model is calibrated in 2 of 35 regime-and-age cells, LEO above 800 km at issue and at half a day; the HPOP epoch state with per-regime P₀ and Q in 5 (section 6). Screening on the uncertainty region relies on calibrated cells. Every screening event also carries the Alfano maximum, the largest probability a spherical covariance of any size gives, which needs no covariance50. It is not a bound for other shapes: at aspect ratios of 3, 10 and 100 to 1,000 the largest probability is about 3, 10 and 57 to 131 times higher, and LEO element-set covariances have aspect ratios of 2.4 to 191.

Covariance propagates through HPOP's state-transition matrix with per-regime process noise, or grows with element-set age in the model of rule 3 (section 6).

2.3Evidence model

An orbit solution is an inference with five contributors:

  1. The object's physics
  2. The dynamics model
  3. The observations
  4. The measurement model
  5. The inference method

A provider's ephemeris or element set already embeds the provider's models and inference, so the catalog treats it as the provider's estimate, not as an observation. Agreement between two products counts as independent confirmation only when their inputs are independent.

Orbit data sit at four processing levels, by analogy with Earth-science data levels. A higher level is more processed, not better: it inherits every flaw below it, and re-epoching adds no evidence. Each input is placed at its level.

Level Content SDN inputs
0 Raw sensor data Detector counts, signal samples, images
1 Calibrated, time-tagged measurements Laser normal points; radio Doppler; optical angles
2 Estimated states and ephemerides Operator ephemerides; GNSS and laser-ranging precise orbits; Vimpel elements; ILRS predictions
3 Theory-specific mean elements fitted to level 1 or 2 OMMs from Space-Track and SpaceMapper; CelesTrak's supplemental GP; SDN's SGP4 companions

A solution states what produced it:

Field Content
State and validity State or ephemeris, epoch, applicable interval
Inputs The products it rests on and their levels
Dynamics Force model, environment inputs, parameters estimated, considered or assumed
Measurement model Sensor geometry, timing, calibration, biases; unknown when a provider does not state it
Inference Estimator or selection rule, weights, priors
Uncertainty Covariance, its source and its calibration label

Physical validity rests on the measurements of sections 5 to 7.

4Normalization and model dispatch

Vimpel osculating elements

Vimpel's HEO product gives osculating elements in J20005. The standard perifocal rotation turns them into a J2000 state at the same epoch6, with μ = 398,600.4418 km³/s². Vimpel's datefirst file maps its own object numbers to NORAD numbers, with the date each catalog first detected the object; each pair is a candidate link, accepted when the two orbits agree (section 8). An object the file does not map keeps the identifier vimpel:<id>, and no NORAD number is invented7. Velocity differenced from 600 s position samples departs from the analytic velocity by up to 1.1 km/s, so it serves only as a diagnostic.

AOE element sets

AOE's KY sets are SGP4 mean elements: the catalog evaluates them through the same SGP4 as Space-Track's OMMs, under the NORAD number AOE gives, or under aoe:<id> where it gives none. E15 scores them against independent orbits beside Space-Track's, under a plan fixed before its test window E15. On 11 October 2026, its first training day:

The handoff contract

An OMM holds SGP4 mean elements, not osculating ones. The catalog evaluates SGP4 at zero elapsed time, transforms position and velocity from TEME to GCRF at the same instant, and starts HPOP from that state8. The rotation uses the IAU 2006/2000A precession-nutation and the equation of the equinoxes. Against the GMST82 and IAU 1976/1980 rotations of Vallado et al.8, this convention moves positions by 2.0–2.6 m in LEO and 12–16 m at GEO; the catalog uses it throughout. SGP4's drag term B* becomes the ballistic parameter

B=CDAm=2B∗ρ0B = \frac{C_D A}{m} = \frac{2B^*}{\rho_0}B=mCD​A​=ρ0​2B∗​

with ρ₀ from Spacetrack Report No. 39; 0.01 m²/kg stands in where B* ≤ 0, and Cr·A/m is 0.02 m²/kg10. The handoff is valid; whether it helps is measured in section 5.3.

Levels of measurement of OMM elements

OMM fields mix nominal (catalog number), ordinal (element-set number), interval (epoch), ratio (mean motion) and circular (node, perigee, mean anomaly) scales, so the pipeline computes on SGP4-evaluated states, never on raw fields. The legacy two-line format quantizes angles to 0.0001°, about 12 m along track near 6,900 km: a resolution, not an accuracy.

Dispatch, frames and time

Each record is routed by its declared theory: mean elements to SGP4, osculating elements to the two-body conversion, states and ephemerides to frame transforms. Osculating elements offered to SGP4 are refused as a model mismatch. Frames follow the IERS Conventions (2010): IAU 2006/2000A, CIO-based, through ERFA, with IERS Earth orientation11. TEME, EME2000, GCRF and ITRF stay distinct; epochs are UTC and integration runs on TT.

SDN's own OMMs

SDN fits its own OMMs to any source's ephemeris: operator ephemerides (SpaceX's MEME files, CCSDS OEM, Earth-fixed files), GNSS orbits (SP3), laser-ranging predictions (CPF) and its own HPOP solutions. Each fit gives two element sets over a declared interval, each scored on a held-out stretch before release: an SGP4 set with B*, which any SGP4 implementation reads, and an SGP4-XP set with its ballistic (BTERM) and radiation (AGOM) coefficients, propagated by the Astrodynamics Standards library52. CelesTrak's supplemental GP fits operator ephemerides with SGP4 the same way48, Spacebook fits extended-perturbation element sets to its own orbits51, and the 18th Space Defense Squadron's eGP fits SP states49. The fit RMS that CelesTrak publishes for each set is the benchmark.

5.5Maneuver detection

The detector compares the trajectories of consecutive element sets at one instant, whatever propagator answers its queries: a crossing test and a robust likelihood-ratio test for steps in orbit-averaged energy and orbit plane, merged by default10. It builds on element-set time series30 and the generalized likelihood ratio for jumps31. Thresholds came from the ISS in 2020–2024; every other window was read once. A planned ISS burn counts as truth only when NASA's next trajectory shows it executed; one 2021 plan was not.

Agreement with Orekit

HPOP's Orekit 13.1 suite44 holds 72 cases on five orbits (400 km, 700 km sun-synchronous, GPS, geostationary, Molniya), each 24 hours, compared hourly on identical states, constants, daily Earth-orientation rows and space weather10. HPOP 1.3.0 (module SHA-256 9c24bf0e) integrates with RK78 at a relative tolerance of 1e-13 (point mass at 1e-15) and the steps named below; Orekit with Dormand–Prince 8(5,3) at 1e-14 and steps of at most 10 s:

Force set Steps of at most 300 s Steps of at most 30 s
Point mass 0.6 mm 0.6 mm
Gravity field, third bodies, radiation pressure, tides, relativity, in-track thrust 1.9 cm 9.9 mm
With drag (NRLMSISE-00 or JB2008, daily space weather, drag rate) 2.5 cm 4.2 mm

At steps of up to 300 s, HPOP's own integration error is most of the difference: 1.8 cm over a day at 400 km (2.6 cm with JB2008 drag) and 1.6 cm at 700 km, against 0.03 mm for GPS satellites. With steps of 30 s the rows no longer depend on the step (a tolerance of 1e-15 with steps of 10 s moves them by under 0.2 mm), and the largest difference is in Molniya, 9.9 mm; 400 km and 700 km agree to 4.2 mm with drag and 1.7 mm without, and GPS and geostationary orbits to 0.2 mm. The rest is Earth orientation: Orekit applies the IERS Conventions' tidal and libration corrections to polar motion and UT111, and HPOP interpolates the daily rows. Against Orekit without those corrections the differences at 30 s are 0.6 mm for point mass, 1.1 mm without drag and 3.9 mm with it, and 0.6 mm in Molniya.

State-transition matrices agree within 2e-5 and parameter sensitivities within 1.5e-4 (relative), at steps of up to 300 s. Integrating Jacchia's diffusion equations numerically reproduces the closed forms of Jacchia-Roberts45 to 1.6e-4.

Time and ephemerides were checked against ERFA 2.0.157 and CSPICE N006758. TAI−UTC agrees exactly, TT from UTC within 5.6e-12 s, and the celestial-to-terrestrial rotation at the instant the force model evaluates within 0.0005 mas. Sun and Moon positions from DE440 agree with CSPICE exactly at the same TDB date. Against JPL Horizons59, which serves DE441, HPOP's Sun and Moon differ by 1.5 m and 2.4 m. CSPICE with DE440 differs from Horizons by 1.4 m for Venus and 1.3 m for the Jupiter barycenter, so these differences come from the two ephemeris releases and their time realization.

HPOP's classical frames (mean and true of date, TEME, and the Earth-fixed chain used when no Earth-orientation rows are given) were compared with ERFA's IAU 1976/1980 chain through pyerfa 2.0.1, under the same conventions (UT1 equal to UTC, no polar motion or frame bias), at 10,000 epochs in all 64 frame pairs: positions agree within 3.0e-8 m at geostationary radius and velocities within 3.7e-12 m/s. Against Orekit 13.1's classical frames, mean of date, true of date and TEME differ by 0.011–0.019 mas, 0.35–0.64 mm in LEO and 2.2–4.0 mm at geostationary radius; Orekit composes precession from the IERS 1996 polynomials, ERFA from IAU 1976's. The rotation the force model applies, the IAU 2006/2000A CIO chain with daily IERS rows, agrees with ERFA within 0.0005 mas and with Orekit within 0.0006 mas given the same Earth-orientation values; Orekit's cubic interpolation of the daily rows against HPOP's linear interpolation adds up to 0.26 mas (8.7 mm in LEO). SPICE's ITRF93 differs from the CIO chain by 8.9 mas, a spread between the two terrestrial-frame realizations.

The SGP4 module was compared with Vallado's SGP4 verification set8 (666 samples, near-Earth and deep space). At the same minutes since epoch, the shipped module agrees with Vallado's own code within 0.11 mm and 7.2e-8 m/s. At the requested epoch, rounding the request to one double-precision Julian date dominates the difference: up to 0.15 m near Earth and 9.7 m on Vallado's longest deep-space arcs. 17 of the 666 samples exceed the sum of the separately measured parts by up to 0.109 mm. The SGP4-XP module returns the Space Force library's states52 exactly, the same doubles, on 640 SGP4 and SGP4-XP sets at seven times each.

8Association and selection

Association asks whether records describe one object; selection picks a solution for an epoch, interval and use; estimation derives a new one. Candidates come from provider crosswalks, such as Vimpel's datefirst file of Vimpel and NORAD numbers, international designators and native-identifier histories; names and bare numbers are not join keys. Compatibility is tested on normalized ephemerides over shared intervals. Each candidate link is compatible, rejected, ambiguous or insufficient, and accepted links stay reversible. Selection takes, per regime and horizon, the source with the lowest median error on a training window of at least 30 samples E3.

Rule Measured Evidence

11What conjunction assessment consumes

Conjunction screening50 takes from one catalog snapshot, per object:

Input From the catalog
Trajectory The OMM for SGP4, or one propagator's trajectory; one propagator per run
Covariance Supplied by the operator, fitted, or modeled (section 6), with its calibration label
Hard-body radius Supplied, half the catalog size, from radar cross-section, or the default, with its basis

An OMM supplies no covariance, so the catalog attaches one by the rule of section 2.2: built from the source's element-set errors against independent orbits and calibrated by regime and age, with its calibration label. Every event also carries the Alfano maximum, the largest probability a spherical covariance of any size gives, which needs no covariance. Products built from public OMMs are advisory: NASA's conjunction assessment handbook (Vol. 2, App. E) and the Space-Track user agreement state that public element sets alone must not drive conjunction assessment decisions49,53.
