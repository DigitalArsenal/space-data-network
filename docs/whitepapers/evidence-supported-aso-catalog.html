1Summary

Space Data Network (SDN) builds a catalog of anthropogenic space objects (ASOs) from public and login-only sources. It keeps every provider's product as an attributed record and, object by object, publishes the solution with the best measured accuracy for the intended use, with its uncertainty and lineage. Accuracy is measured against independent precise orbits, under plans fixed before the test data were read R1.

Object class Catalog product Measured (median)
GPS satellites Analysis-centre orbit, propagated 3.5 cm at issue, 30 m a day later
Satellites with laser ranging High-precision orbit propagation (HPOP) fitted to the ranges 7 m at the fit, 42 m after 7 days
Satellites with operator ephemerides The operator's ephemeris Public element sets (OMMs) lie 0.6–5 km from it
Objects known only from public OMMs SGP4 on the OMM 0.3–1.6 km at issue

HPOP's force models agree with Orekit within 1.6 cm over a day, and from precise states they leave a median 4.8 m against laser-ranging orbits after a day. Started from an OMM's own epoch state, HPOP is never better than SGP4: the start state, not the force model, limits it. None of the density corrections tested improves 1–3-day orbit prediction in its pre-registered test, and covariances proved realistic on held-out data in few regimes and horizons.

Three results go beyond published work and are marked [NEW] where they enter: GPS element-set correction fails on an unseen orbital plane (section 5.3); a density correction of the kind the Space Force's drag model uses, solved from Planet's public states, is scored on satellites that did not calibrate it (section 5.4); consecutive OMM errors are strongly correlated, and four published covariance constructions are overconfident (section 6). Pre-registered hypotheses that failed are reported as not supported.

2Evidence model and records

An orbit solution is an inference with five contributors: the object's physics, the dynamics model, the observations, the measurement model and the inference method. A provider's ephemeris or element set already embeds the provider's models and inference, so the catalog keeps it as an attributed provider claim, not as an observation. Agreement between products counts as independent confirmation only when their lineage is independent; unknown lineage is recorded as unknown.

Orbit data sit at four processing levels, by analogy with Earth-science data levels. A higher level is more processed, not better: it inherits every flaw below it, and re-epoching adds no evidence. Every source record declares its level.

Level Content SDN inputs
0 Raw sensor data Detector counts, signal samples, images
1 Calibrated, time-tagged measurements Laser normal points; radio Doppler; optical angles
2 Estimated states and ephemerides Operator ephemerides; GNSS and laser-ranging precise orbits; Vimpel elements; ILRS predictions
3 Theory-specific mean elements fitted to level 1 or 2 OMMs from Space-Track and SpaceMapper; CelesTrak's supplemental GP; SDN's SGP4 companions

A source record keeps the provider, native identifier, edition, SHA-256 of the bytes, product type, epoch, time scale and frame; observation time, solution epoch, issue time and retrieval time stay distinct. A solution record specifies:

Field Content
State and validity State or ephemeris, epoch, applicable interval
Evidence and lineage Source records, their levels and dependencies, editions
Dynamics Force model, environment inputs, parameters estimated, considered or assumed
Measurement model Sensor geometry, timing, calibration, biases; unknown when a provider does not state it
Inference Estimator or selection rule, weights, priors, software version
Uncertainty Covariance or none, its source and its calibration label

Hashes establish byte identity and signatures establish attribution; physical validity rests on the measurements of sections 5 to 7. Identical inputs, module binaries and policy reproduce the same decisions.

4Normalization and model dispatch

Vimpel osculating elements

Vimpel's HEO product gives osculating elements in J2000 R5. The standard perifocal rotation turns them into a J2000 state at the same epoch R6, with μ = 398,600.4418 km³/s², under the identifier vimpel:<id> and no invented NORAD number R7. Velocity differenced from 600 s position samples departs from the analytic velocity by up to 1.1 km/s, so it serves only as a diagnostic.

The handoff contract

An OMM holds SGP4 mean elements, not osculating ones. The catalog evaluates SGP4 at zero elapsed time, transforms position and velocity from TEME to GCRF at the same instant, and starts HPOP from that state R8. SGP4's drag term B* becomes the ballistic parameter

B=CDAm=2B∗ρ0B = \frac{C_D A}{m} = \frac{2B^*}{\rho_0}B=mCD​A​=ρ0​2B∗​

with ρ₀ from Spacetrack Report No. 3 R9; 0.01 m²/kg stands in where B* ≤ 0, and Cr·A/m is 0.02 m²/kg R10. The handoff is valid; whether it helps is measured in section 5.3.

Levels of measurement of OMM elements

OMM fields mix nominal (catalog number), ordinal (element-set number), interval (epoch), ratio (mean motion) and circular (node, perigee, mean anomaly) scales, so the pipeline computes on SGP4-evaluated states, never on raw fields. The legacy two-line format quantizes angles to 0.0001°, about 12 m along track near 6,900 km: a resolution, not an accuracy.

Dispatch, frames and time

Each record is routed by its declared theory: mean elements to SGP4, osculating elements to the two-body conversion, states and ephemerides to frame transforms. Osculating elements offered to SGP4 are refused as a model mismatch. Frames follow the IERS Conventions (2010): IAU 2006/2000A, CIO-based, through ERFA, with IERS Earth orientation R11. TEME, EME2000, GCRF and ITRF stay distinct; epochs are UTC and integration runs on TT.

5.5Maneuver detection

The detector compares the trajectories of consecutive element sets at one instant, whatever propagator answers its queries: a crossing test and a robust likelihood-ratio test for steps in orbit-averaged energy and orbit plane, merged by default R10. It builds on element-set time series R30 and the generalized likelihood ratio for jumps R31. Thresholds came from the ISS in 2020–2024; every other window was read once. A planned ISS burn counts as truth only when NASA's next trajectory shows it executed; one 2021 plan was not.

Held-out class, 2025–2026 Truth Found False alarms

6Uncertainty

Covariance propagates through HPOP's state-transition matrix Φ with a declared white-acceleration process noise Q R10:

P(t)=Φ(t,t0) P0 Φ(t,t0)T+Q(t)P(t) = \Phi(t, t_0)\, P_0\, \Phi(t, t_0)^{\mathsf{T}} + Q(t)P(t)=Φ(t,t0​)P0​Φ(t,t0​)T+Q(t)

Φ supplies neither P₀ nor Q; they come from a fit, a provider or an empirical model. Realism is scored against independent precise orbits by d2=eTP−1ed^2 = \mathbf{e}^{\mathsf{T}} P^{-1} \mathbf{e}d2=eTP−1e for a position error e, against χ² with 3 degrees of freedom: a covariance is realistic when the mean of d²/3 lies in [0.8, 1.25] and its 95 % coverage in [0.93, 0.97], the rule of E14 after Zaidi and Hejduk R32.

Agreement with Orekit

HPOP's Orekit 13.1 suite R44 holds 72 cases on five orbits (400 km, 700 km sun-synchronous, GPS, geostationary, Molniya), each 24 hours, compared hourly on identical states, constants, Earth orientation and space weather R10:

Force set Largest 3D difference
Point mass 0.6 mm
Gravity field, third bodies, radiation pressure, tides, relativity, in-track thrust Under 1 cm
With drag (NRLMSISE-00 or JB2008, daily space weather, drag rate) 1.6 cm

State-transition matrices agree within 2e-5 and parameter sensitivities within 1.4e-4 (relative). What remains is integration error on both sides, about 1e-11 of the distance flown. Integrating Jacchia's diffusion equations numerically reproduces the closed forms of Jacchia-Roberts R45 to 1.6e-4.

HPOP's resident path, which holds the catalog in memory, reproduces its execution path to 0.052 mm over four days, at 0.645 s per object-day on one thread R10.

8Association and selection

Association asks whether records describe one object; selection picks a solution for an epoch, interval and use; estimation derives a new one. Candidates come from provider crosswalks, international designators and native-identifier histories, Vimpel's datefirst fields included; names and bare numbers are not join keys. Compatibility is tested on normalized ephemerides over shared intervals. Each candidate link is compatible, rejected, ambiguous or insufficient, and accepted links stay reversible. Selection takes, per regime and horizon, the source with the lowest median error on a training window of at least 30 samples E3.

Rule Measured Evidence
Selection by evidence GPS: 3.5 cm at issue (section 5.1) E3

9SP message interchange

The VCM carries an SP state, its force settings and an equinoctial covariance R39. SDN's adapter reads a VCM into an HPOP request and writes the result back as a VCM, transforming the covariance with an exact Jacobian computed by forward-mode dual numbers R47. The format states no covariance units, and no public definition was found. Four messages settle them: the survey's ISS sample and SP messages for a geostationary, a GPS and an eccentric (e = 0.59) orbit.

Covariance rows Reading Evidence
Mean motion Relative, dn/n; covariance scaled by max(1, weighted RMS)² Every printed U, V and W sigma reproduced within 1 %; no absolute unit does (radians per 1000 s misses two radial sigmas by 1.7 and 2.0 times)
B and AGOM Fractions of the coefficients (1.5–9.7 %) The GPS message against ESA's final orbit for one day: radial and in-track RMS 0.46 and 0.64 of their sigmas, cross-track 1.27; read as printed, the sigmas overstate the error 7 to 20 times

A round trip through VCM text in SP's number format recovers the covariance to 2e-5 of its sigmas. A VCM naming JBH09 runs as JB2008 on SET's indices, which the caller supplies.

10Publication and reproduction

Publication is transactional: acquire and hash the bytes, verify, normalize, validate, sign a manifest, then announce the new catalog head. Consumers read one complete, immutable snapshot; each update names the object, the prior and new solutions, the epoch, the recipe and the provenance. Content addressing and independent replication keep a snapshot retrievable from any node that holds it.

An accepted numerical solution can carry an SGP4 companion: mean elements fitted to it over a declared interval and scored on a held-out stretch before release, as CelesTrak's supplemental GP fits operator ephemerides R48 and the 18th Space Defense Squadron's eGP fits SP states R49. The fit RMS that CelesTrak publishes for each set is the benchmark.

Each experiment's numbers come from a run manifest: the repository and module commits, each module's WebAssembly SHA-256 and the SHA-256 of every input; reports are generated from metrics R1. The experiments' public site runs the same modules in a web browser: a V1 seed reproduces the committed result exactly, and any Orekit case re-runs from its exact request. Space-Track element sets, SpaceX and Planet files and SET's indices are cited by URL and SHA-256, not copied.

12What conjunction assessment consumes

Conjunction screening R50 takes from one catalog snapshot, per object:

Input From the catalog
Trajectory The OMM for SGP4, or one propagator's trajectory; one propagator per run
Covariance Supplied by the operator, fitted, or modeled (section 6), with its calibration label
Hard-body radius Supplied, half the catalog size, from radar cross-section, or the default, with its basis
Provenance Snapshot, source records and recipe

An OMM supplies no covariance. A collision probability needs relative-state uncertainty with independent calibration and stated correlation, geometry and hard-body radius; without it the screen reports only the covariance-free maximum probability.
